# Journal: Nature Scientific Data — submission guidelines

This manuscript (`manuscript.tex`) is a Data Descriptor submission to
*Scientific Data*. Keep the following in mind for any writing, editing,
or review of this manuscript.

## Editorial scope (from the journal's Guide to Authors)

Scientific Data publishes descriptions of open research datasets,
written to promote reuse — not to report whether the dataset supports
a hypothesis or conclusion. Concretely:

- **No hypothesis-testing framing.** Do not present the dataset as
  evidence for or against a scientific claim. Stay focused on how the
  dataset was created and how to reuse it.
- **No imposed interpretation.** Do not draw conclusions from the data
  or suggest what the data "shows" about the world. Referees may ask
  for removal of any analysis or claim that goes beyond describing the
  dataset itself.
- **No preliminary/weakly-supported results.** This is not the venue
  for exploratory findings — only for the dataset and the rigor of the
  process that produced it.
- **Impact/novelty of findings is not a review criterion.** Don't
  frame contributions in terms of novel scientific insight; frame them
  in terms of data quality, rigor of construction, and reusability.
- **Depth is expected**, at a level of detail beyond what's typical
  elsewhere: experimental design, data collection, and processing must
  be described thoroughly enough to support reuse and pass scrutiny —
  flaws here (not weak "impact") are the main basis for rejection.
- **Full data availability required.** Referees must have access to
  the complete dataset as described in the manuscript, and full public
  release via a trusted community data repository is required before
  publication.

## How this shapes the manuscript

- The **Background & Summary**, **Methods**, **Data Records**, and
  **Technical Validation** sections should read as documentation of
  process and rigor, not as a case for a scientific result.
- Any language suggesting the dataset "reveals," "demonstrates," or
  "confirms" something about pluvial flood risk/damages (as opposed to
  about the data-cleaning process itself) is out of scope and should
  be flagged or cut.
- Technical Validation should demonstrate the *correctness and
  reliability of the cleaning/correction pipeline* (e.g. boundary
  match rates, sensitivity of the pluvial date-correction threshold),
  not analyze flood risk trends.
- Data Availability must point to a trusted public repository with the
  full dataset, not just a description — check this before submission.
